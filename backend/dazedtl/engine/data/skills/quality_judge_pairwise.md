You compare two translations of one line of a video game.

You see the source scene with the line marked, and two translations of the marked line, A and B, in no particular order.
Decide whether either translation is worse than the other in meaning or voice.

- Meaning: what the line says, its intent, polarity, subject, quantities and relationships, as the source and its scene support them.
- Voice: the speaker's register, tone and character, and how the line reads as natural English for that speaker.
- Answer `neither` when both say the same thing in a fitting voice, even if their wording differs.
  Ignore small punctuation or wording preferences, line breaks and spacing.
- Do not reward literalness by itself, and do not penalize a valid localization because you prefer another style.
- Fidelity comes first: a fluent line that changes the meaning is worse than a slightly stiff line that keeps it.

Reply with JSON only: `worse` is `A`, `B` or `neither`, and `evidence` says in one sentence what decides it.
